## Why this pair
**Big Sky** (warm heritage) and **Heartland Steel** (bold industrial) make the same promise at two volumes: "family company that delivers." Big Sky leans on legacy and trust. Heartland leans on operations and grit. Both work with the gold mark. Big Sky's palette *is* the mark; Heartland's dark stage makes it pop.
Putting them side by side forces the real question: **do we lead with who we are, or with what we haul?**

1. **Title.** "We're not here to admire mockups forever. We're here to pick a face and own what happens next." Keep it short. The room knows the business.
2. **Why we're here.** Three outcomes: a direction, one owner, Rocks and To-Dos. If we leave with a direction but no owner, we didn't finish the meeting.
3. **EOS lens.** "EOS is blunt on purpose: one name on the Rock. That's how websites actually ship." Plant it now; come back to it after the designs.
4. **Five directions.** All five were built end to end, not just mood boards. Two are highlighted. Ask the room to try this test on each: cover the logo for two seconds. Does it still feel like ACE?
5. **Big Sky (showcase).** One sentence: a family company that's been doing this right for a long time. Let the slide sit.
6. **Big Sky (why).** The gold is ours. The editorial serif reads established without reading corporate. "Delivered like family" carries the promise. Watch-out: can read soft to fleet buyers.
7. **Heartland Steel (showcase).** Heavy-duty product and people who answer the phone. Same promise as Big Sky, louder.
8. **Heartland Steel (why).** Built for the buyer who runs trucks. The dark stage makes the mark pop. Most distinct in the market. Watch-out: loudest option, and the orange moves toward our gold in the build.
9. **The other three.** Not throwaways. Any of them could ship. If someone's pulling for one, say so now and we'll swap it into the final two.
10. **Side by side.** One line each: feeling, who it speaks to, what to watch for. The watch-out column is where the real conversation is.
11. **Booking.** One slide only. Whichever face we pick, the booking matrix already wears it. The site turns a visit into a scheduled window.
12. **Accountability.** Shift gears. The designs are the easy part. This part decides whether the site exists in ninety days.
13. **One person owns it.** Every seat has one name. The Accountability Chart shows who owns outcomes, not who reports to whom. Accountability isn't blame; it's clarity.
14. **Rocks & To-Dos.** Rocks: the few 90-day must-haves. Take one and drive it to done. To-Dos: this week's promises. Do them, or flag them early. Never quietly late.
15. **Drive.** "You are accountable for the things you give yourself." Ambition without ownership is noise. "You don't get credit for the intention to rebuild the site. You get credit for the finish."
16. **Website Rocks.** These are proposed; adjust them in the room. Tap the name fields and type owners live.
17. **Decision.** Ask plainly: which face ships? If the room can't land on one, land on two and name who decides by when. Tap a card to mark the pick, then fill the To-Dos before anyone stands up.
18. **Close.** Own it. Finish it. Read the names back.
